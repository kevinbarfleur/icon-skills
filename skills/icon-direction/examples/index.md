# Four different construction decisions

Open each example's preview image before using its source as a reference.
The names are collection study labels, not cleared product names.
The briefs retain the collection's proposed meanings and interpretation risks, not audience-validation claims.
The examples are intentionally small; the complete library is not required.
Sources and previews are synchronized by the repository's `scripts/build-skill-examples.mjs` generator before packaging.
That generator is not needed to use this folder.

## Blue hour: organic, flat, off-center

Files: `bloom/preview.png`, `bloom/master.svg`, `bloom/brief.json`.
Five unequal petals orbit a warm center while a weighted stem pulls the arrangement down.
The source uses a few large filled contours instead of details inside every petal.
Its yellow field and blue flower establish a strong area contrast; the smaller hot center is the focal exception.
Inspect how the petal gaps and the offset center prevent a mechanically repeated rosette.
Transfer the balance principle only when useful, not the flower, palette or number of petals.

## City fold: an object transformed by its meaning

Files: `city-fold/preview.png`, `city-fold/master.svg`, `city-fold/brief.json`.
A map becomes a canyon or building through folded planes and a path crossing them.
The source uses separate warm planes and a cool vertical structure to distinguish surfaces with little geometry.
The large field is deliberately stronger than the internal shading.
Inspect every plane junction: the apparent fold relies on overlap and contrast, not a generic shadow behind the whole icon.
Literal subjects can work when their construction supplies a specific visual idea.

## Pace partner: rhythm within one continuous mass

Files: `studio-pace-partner/preview.png`, `studio-pace-partner/master.svg`, `studio-pace-partner/brief.json`.
A racing ribbon narrows through a quiet interval and widens again.
Direction, relative width and a restrained light opening carry the meaning instead of a runner illustration.
The gradient supports the ribbon's material; it is not a replacement for the silhouette.
An abstract direction still needs an intentional relationship between its parts.
Do not reproduce this ribbon merely because another product has a sequence or rhythm.

## Overlap: the shared region is the subject

Files: `product-07a-overlap/preview.png`, `product-07a-overlap/master.svg`, `product-07a-overlap/brief.json`.
Two speech-like masses meet in a darker shared area.
The outlines and intersection make the conversational relationship legible before the surface highlights are noticed.
Compare the area of the shared region to each outer lobe and the direction of the two tails.
This example uses more dimensional color than Blue hour; both are valid finishes for different intentions.
Never apply translucency or paired speech forms as the default treatment of every collaborative product.
