# Review the image, then correct the source

Inspect `preview-1024.png` and `review.png`, not just the XML or a successful command exit.
Small PNGs are rendered directly at each size, not resized from a screenshot.
The proof sheet uses square masters on two surrounding colors; it does not simulate an operating system or certify platform compliance.
If an image viewer rescales the whole sheet, also open the individual 60 and 29 px files and report the viewing limitation.

## Questions that change a design

- Does the silhouette still have a distinct event at 29 px, or has it collapsed into a generic blob?
- Is the focal area where the brief needs it, or has a highlight or small accent stolen attention?
- Do near contacts become unintended outlines, holes, specks or ambiguous overlaps?
- Does a part that should be behind another actually look behind it at every junction?
- Do thin strokes and counterforms survive at 60 and 29 px?
- Does the visual grammar differ from the other proposed directions and relevant examples?
- Does color give this composition its own hierarchy, or repeat the same muted field, pale object and warm accent as unrelated work?
- On the rendered palette alternatives, which important edge or focal area improves, and what becomes weaker?
- Is the user-facing feeling appropriate even when the written explanation is hidden?

Record observations with a location and consequence.
For example: "The pale joint between the two planes disappears at 29 px, so the fold reads as a stripe; widen the dark separator and rerender."
Avoid evidence-free ratings such as "premium, 9/10."
A technically valid SVG can still be banal or poorly composed.

Keep the before and after when revising.
Explain the specific improvement and any new tradeoff.
If no correction improves the candidate, retain it and say so instead of manufacturing a revision.
For a narrow adaptation, compare source geometry before and after to confirm that only the authorized property changed.

A final technical manifest reports source integrity and output sizes, not an artistic pass.
Artistic review is the agent's observed assessment until a person reviews the work.
Do not upgrade that assessment into audience validation or a promise of reliable quality.
