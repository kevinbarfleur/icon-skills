# Color belongs to this composition

Choose a color structure for the product and its visible idea, rather than applying a pleasant palette after drawing.
This work is automatic when the brief leaves color open; the user does not need to request palette exploration or artistic contrast explicitly.
Supplied brand colors and exact edit requests take precedence, with technical review of the resulting hierarchy still required.
In early fresh-project trials, greenhouse care, budgeting and clothing repair all converged on muted teal, peach and pale yellow.
Their hex values differed, but their color personalities did not.
A recurring arrangement of values and saturation can become a house style even when no exact color is reused.

Each row keeps the same geometry; the first column is the earlier palette.
The following columns explore different distributions of lightness, chroma and focal emphasis.
In the greenhouse, the pale-field treatment concentrates color inside the panes instead of making the bed another warm focal area.
In the budget mark, the neutral treatment shows that separation can rely on value without a vivid notification-like dot.
In the repair study, the bright workwear field competes with the seam, whereas the dark red treatment gives the pale seam priority.
These are observed tradeoffs, not approved identities or a bank of palettes to reuse.
The budget silhouette remains generic in every column; palette research does not resolve that separate problem.

## Start with hierarchy

Identify the part whose visibility carries the idea: a joint, opening, continuous mass, protected area or another actual feature.
Decide what should attract attention first and what should support it.
Include the background in that decision; it often occupies more area than any object.
Assign roles to color areas, not just a list of swatches.

For a visible repair, contrast might concentrate on the thread while the cloth stays close in value.
For a quiet financial reserve, the separated part might be denser or less chromatic than the available area, rather than becoming another orange notification dot.
These are possible relationships to test, not prescribed colors for those industries.
Do not assert universal meanings such as "blue means trust" as evidence for a choice.
Tie a proposal to the brief, material, context and what the rendered image actually emphasizes.

## Explore different color structures

When the palette is open, compare a few materially different treatments on the same geometry.
One may use a pale field and a dense mark; another a chromatic field with a restrained object; another a narrow hue range with a sharp value interval.
Choose hypotheses that suit the particular composition, not those three recipes every time.
Change the distribution of lightness, chroma and occupied area when useful, not merely the hue of the same warm accent.
A neutral or monochromatic treatment may have more personality than several vivid colors.
Pastels and dark fields remain legitimate when deliberately chosen.
Replacing every muted palette with neon is another uniform house style, not a solution.

Keep the geometry fixed for this comparison so a shape improvement is not mistaken for a color improvement.
Swatches alone cannot reveal whether a large colored background overwhelms a small focal feature.
View the actual candidates beside one another at app size.
If available, compare unrelated recent work too, looking for repeated color hierarchy rather than exact hex matches.
Do not fetch a whole library just to perform that comparison.

## Inspect the relationships that matter

Check the important adjacent surfaces at 60 and 29 px.
Do they remain distinct, and does the focal element keep its intended priority?
Two lively hues can still merge at small size if their values are close.
A very bright field can compete with a fine gesture even when all parts are technically visible.
An accent can suggest a warning, notification or decoration the product did not intend.
Use a grayscale proof when it helps diagnose value structure, without requiring every decorative hue difference to survive grayscale.
Do not apply text-contrast thresholds as an artistic quality score for a logo.

For relief or material-based treatments, preserve the direction of light and the relation between lit and shaded surfaces.
Independent random recoloring of highlights and shadows can destroy the material.
Keep related expressions of one identity coherent unless a distinct alternative identity was requested.
Diversity is needed between unrelated products, not arbitrary disagreement within one brand.

## Select and explain briefly

Name the visible advantage of the chosen treatment over an alternative and one remaining tradeoff.
"The dark cloth recedes and the red seam leads" is a reviewable observation.
"A premium, confident palette" does not explain a decision.
Retain the alternatives and their editable sources, including a rejected vivid version when saturation alone did not help.
A palette cannot rescue an uninteresting or misleading silhouette; report that limitation separately.
