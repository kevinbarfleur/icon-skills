# Decisions from the collection

## Reuse the meaning, not the template

A preserved botanical specimen inside a book can suggest keeping a meaningful encounter.
A different object can occupy that relationship when preservation is still the product's promise.
A financial dashboard or a climbing journal may need an entirely different event.
Repeated subject swaps previously reduced the collection's quality and made unrelated products look like one company.

## Inspect every expression

True estimate originally used a gradient whose terminal color matched the measuring jaw.
Flattening the gradient merged two important parts in the Drawn and Relief expressions.
A separate copper plane restored the relationship between instrument and measured object.

Pale SVG strokes became dark ink in the historic drawing renderer.
On a dark surface, those marks disappeared.
The newer compositions opt into preserving stroke colors, while previously approved artwork retains its earlier behavior.

## Compare with neighbors

Clicks into place originally repeated the cut-ring construction of Good margin.
An asymmetrical abacus made the learning concept independent.
Ready range originally resembled the hammock in Resting place.
A diagonal band with two grips replaced that construction.
The distinctiveness check belongs beside the individual finish review.

## Know what the evidence establishes

Product pages establish that a workflow exists.
The proposed visual meaning, audience recognition and willingness to pay remain hypotheses.
Use dated primary sources for specific workflow claims, not invented market-size figures.
